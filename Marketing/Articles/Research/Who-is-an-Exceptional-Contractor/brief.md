## The job of this post

Set the standard. Before we ask anyone to nominate (1 Sep), answer the prior question: *what does exceptional actually look like on the contractor side?* Move the reader from "good contractor = delivered on time/budget" to something richer (judgement, behaviour under pressure, the relationship — not just the deliverable).

## Angle (one idea only)

Excellence isn't the absence of problems — it's what the contractor does **when things go wrong**. The award exists to recognise that, not the glossy on-time case study.
