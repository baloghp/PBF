## Hook — portfolio review / digitisation without the right questions

**External spend is most of the cost base, and still under-examined.**

McKinsey, *A new era for procurement* (2023): "Despite typically accounting for **50 to 80 percent** of a company’s cost base, external spend often receives less attention than sales- or productivity-improvement efforts." Same piece: of 35 CPOs surveyed, most said they lacked platforms for integrated real-time processing, and **less than 20%** of available procurement data was currently used.

- https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/operations/our-insights/a-new-era-for-procurement-value-creation-across-the-supply-chain

**Dashboards measure the last job, not whether to continue.**

Gartner, *Supplier Relationship Management* (citing 2023 Supplier Quality Management Survey): the most common challenge in improving supplier performance is that quality metrics are **more focused on fixing problems than on anticipating them**. Leading indicators they recommend include responsiveness, change-control management, extent of training programmes, and employee turnover — i.e. not just OTD/cost.

- https://www.gartner.com/en/supply-chain/topics/supplier-relationship-management

**Classic supplier-selection literature already said "not just price."**

Dickson (1966), *Journal of Purchasing*: 23 criteria; quality, delivery and past performance ranked above price. Ho, Xu & Dey (2010), *EJOR* 202(1): most-used evaluation criteria remain quality, delivery, cost/price, then capability, financial stability, flexibility. Background only.

## 1. Project success (outcomes)

**Why it matters.** Delivery vs baseline is the hygiene layer. The award's extra ask is lasting impact after handover. Dashboards stop at the acceptance certificate; clients live with what is left.

**Headline.** Flyvbjerg & Gardner (2023), database of **16,000+** major projects (20+ fields, 136 countries): **47.9%** finish on budget; **8.5%** on budget *and* on time; **0.5%** on budget, on time, *and* with the promised benefits. Same authors in HBR (Jan 2023, "How Frank Gehry Delivers On Time and On Budget"): "a mere 0.5% were completed on time and on budget and produced the expected benefits."

- Book: *How Big Things Get Done* (Currency/Pan Macmillan, 2023)
- HBR: https://hbr.org/2023/01/how-frank-gehry-delivers-on-time-and-on-budget
- Publisher restatement (47.9 / 8.5 / 0.5): https://www.panmacmillan.com/blogs/general/the-worlds-most-successful-big-projects

**Older iron law (megaprojects only).** Flyvbjerg, *PMJ* 2014 / Oxford Handbook intro: "nine out of ten" have cost overruns; "over budget, over time, under benefits, over and over again." DOI: 10.1002/pmj.21409. Prefer the 2023 16k-project numbers; they include benefits, which is the point of this criterion.

**PMI on value vs triple constraint.** *Project Success* 2024/2025: respondents rate whether the project "delivered value that was worth the effort and expense." 2024: **48%** successful, **40%** mixed, **12%** failure (n ≈ 5,751). Global Net Project Success Score 36 (2024) / 37 (2025). https://www.pmi.org/-/media/pmi/documents/public/pdf/learning/thought-leadership/project_success_2025_final.pdf

**Benefits after close.** PMI *Sustain Benefits to Optimize Business Value*: only **17%** of organisations report high benefits-realisation maturity; high-maturity orgs see **50% more** projects meeting original goals and **69% more** meeting/exceeding forecasted ROI. Use as supporting, not the headline.

**Waste (optional, older).** PMI Pulse 2018: **9.9%** of every dollar wasted to poor project performance = **$99 million per $1 billion**. Pulse 2021 updated waste to **9.4%**. Dated; Flyvbjerg is sharper for "after handover."

**Caveat.** Flyvbjerg is large/major projects, not every contractor SOW. Safe line: *hitting the baseline is rare enough; delivering the benefit you paid for is rarer*.

## 2. Agility & adaptability

**Why it matters.** Change is the job. Contractors who cannot run change without chaos lose schedule and margin — and take the client with them.

**Headline.** FMI *What High Performers Do Differently in Project Execution* (2026, AWCI reprint): firms with a **highly consistent change-order process meet or exceed schedules ~80%** of the time, vs **~65%** for moderate/minimal consistency. Specialty contractors: **87%** vs **64%** hit profit-margin targets. GCs: **68%** vs **61%**.

- https://www.awci.org/wp-content/uploads/Project-Management-Survey-Part-2.pdf

**Academic.** Alshdiefat et al., *J. Constr. Eng. Manage.* (ASCE, 2022): change-order management factors explained **R² = 0.665** of overall project performance (n = 334). Strongest loadings: communication/relationships, dispute resolution, financial issues. DOI: 10.1061/(ASCE)CO.1943-7862.0002350. One study, construction — don't universalise the 66.5%.

**PMI.** Pulse 2021 appendix: **poor change management** named by **24%** as a primary cause of project failure; **change in organisation's priorities** 41%; **change in project objectives** 38%.

**Caveat.** FMI is contractor self-report. Safe line is the 80/65 schedule split, already in v2.

## 3. Commercial model (liquidity)

**Why it matters.** Price realism and when the money actually moves. **Framing for this post is contractor-side:** was the bid honest (neither lowballed nor padded), and did their own supply chain get paid? The client-squeeze version is true but belongs in a different post — here it reads as a lecture to the reader.

**Headline — lowball bidding is a deferred claim.** Petter Matthews (Executive Director, CoST International Secretariat), writing on weforum.org (Feb 2016): the UK "deliberately moved away from lowest price bidding during the last 10-15 years" because "it was not delivering cost savings. In fact, evidence showed that lowest price bidding was more likely to result in time and cost overruns … This is because lowest price bidding encourages contractors to bid for work at unrealistically low levels. It then becomes difficult for them to maintain standards and make profits, the quality of their work falls and they become more likely to make unjustified claims, delay payments to subcontractors and indulge in corrupt behaviour to reduce their losses." Same piece: the 2015 World Bank procurement framework allows award on criteria other than price for the first time.

- https://www.weforum.org/stories/2016/02/why-is-the-construction-industry-so-corrupt-and-what-can-we-do-about-it/
- **Attribution care:** this is a signed contributor piece; WEF's own footer says the views are the author's alone. Say "the UK public sector moved away from lowest-price tendering because it wasn't saving money" — do **not** say "the World Economic Forum found."
- Overpricing/padding: no equivalent hard source. Keep it as an assertion ("the same problem wearing a nicer suit"), not a claim with a number behind it.

**Headline — insolvency.** Official statistics, England & Wales, Insolvency Service commentary for **December 2025**: in the **12 months to November 2025**, construction had the most company insolvencies of any industry — **3,950**, **17%** of cases with industry captured.

- https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/company-insolvencies-december-2025/commentary-company-insolvency-statistics-december-2025

**v2 correction:** the draft currently says "year to December 2025." The 3,950 / 17% line in that release is **12 months to November 2025**. Fix if we keep the number.

**Headline — late payment.** Department for Business and Trade payment-practices data, reported by BCIS: large construction businesses paid **14%** of invoices late in 2025 (**one in seven**); median **33 days** to pay (unchanged from 2024). OSBC citing DBT: late payments cost the UK economy almost **£11 billion** a year; businesses owed an estimated **£26 billion** at any time.

- https://www.bcis.co.uk/news/large-construction-firms-paid-one-in-seven-invoices-late-in-2025/
- https://www.smallbusinesscommissioner.gov.uk/about-osbc/

**Client-side mechanism.** Rabbet *Construction Payments Report* (2024): if the contractor goes bankrupt mid-project, the client pays in halt + replacement cost. Slow payment is a **client risk**.

**Caveat.** Insolvency ranking is UK construction; the criterion applies across industries. Use as the vivid example of what unfair cash flow does, not as "all contractors." Do not cite Marketinvoice's "~30 days beyond terms" unless we re-verify against DBT (median days-to-pay is 33, which is not the same as days-*late*).

## 4. Legal soundness

**Why it matters.** A contract that looks tough and does not match how you work — or that nobody read — is a dispute with a date.

**Headline.** Arcadis *14th Annual Construction Disputes Report* (2024, "Construction Disputes in the Digital Age"): North America average dispute value **$43.0 million** (was **$42.8 million** in 2022 / 13th report). Top causes, two years running: **errors and/or omissions in the contract documents** (tied with) **owner/contractor/subcontractor failing to understand and/or comply with its contractual obligations**. Third: poorly drafted / incomplete / unsubstantiated claims.

- 2023 write-up: https://www.constructiondive.com/news/the-dotted-line-how-to-avoid-skyrocketing-dispute-costs/691927/
- 2024 PDF: https://media.arcadis.com/-/media/project/arcadiscom/com/expertise/global/contract-solutions/14th-annual-construction-disputes-report-2024.pdf

**Academic.** Koç (2022), *ECAM*: contract incompleteness (deficiency, inconsistency, ambiguity) as a dispute driver; top causal factors include ambiguity in enforceability and significant scope amendments.

MDPI *Buildings* systematic review (52 papers, 2000–2024): most frequently cited dispute cause is **ambiguous, contradictory, or incomplete contract documents** (32 occurrences). https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/16/5/944

**Caveat.** Arcadis is construction / North America. PBF criterion is broader (law, jurisdiction, warranty/liability, privity, **fit of contracting approach to collaboration model**). Research supports "complete + understood + suited to how you actually work."

## 5. Interface and governance

**Why it matters.** Who provides what, who decides, who escalates. If the RACI only lived on a kickoff slide, the meeting will argue whose delay it was.

**Headline.** Shen, Tang, Wang, Duffield, Hui & Zhang, *Managing Interfaces in Large-Scale Projects: The Roles of Formal Governance and Partnering*, *J. Constr. Eng. Manage.* — 85 international large-scale projects. **Formal governance is a dominant determinant of interface-management performance**; partnering and boundary activities also matter; IM performance **positively correlated with quality, cost and schedule**. Formal governance and partnering reinforce each other.

- DOI: 10.1061/(ASCE)CO.1943-7862.0002101

**Related.** Eriksson et al., *IJPM* 2023 (102 infrastructure dyads): contractual (coordinative) supply-chain integration sets the stage; **collaborative** integration is what moves performance. Contract alone is not enough. DOI: 10.1016/j.ijproman.2023.102479

Joslin & Müller, *IJPM* 34(4) 2016 (n = 254): project governance has a **small but significant** correlation with project success; **stakeholder-oriented** governance (trust + outcome control) correlates with all five success dimensions, strongest with *future potential*. DOI: 10.1016/j.ijproman.2016.01.008. Don't overclaim the size of the effect.

**Caveat.** No clean single percentage like "X% of failures." Safe line is qualitative, as in v2: when the boundary is real, cost and time tend to hold.

## 6. Risk management (cross-corporate)

**Why it matters.** Not the internal risk log. The risks that sit *between* two companies: insolvency cascade, subcontractor, IP/data, cyber, reputation. You cannot outsource that.

**Headline — cyber (one cross-corporate channel).** IBM *Cost of a Data Breach Report 2025* (Ponemon; 600 organisations, Mar 2024–Feb 2025): **supply chain compromise** surged to the **second most prevalent** initial attack vector (**15%**) and **second costliest** at **USD 4.91 million** (behind malicious insider at USD 4.92 million). Global average breach cost USD 4.44 million.

- IBM newsroom: https://newsroom.ibm.com/2025-07-30-ibm-report-13-of-organizations-reported-breaches-of-ai-models-or-applications,-97-of-which-reported-lacking-proper-ai-access-controls
- Full report (Ponemon/IBM)

**Do not use:** "30% of breaches involved a third party / doubled YoY." That line is circulating on secondary blogs. In the IBM PDF, 30% refers to other cuts (multi-environment storage; AI supply-chain incidents), not the share of all breaches that are third-party.

**Headline — project failure.** PMI Pulse 2021 appendix (n = 3,950): **ineffective risk management** selected by **27%** as a primary cause of project failure (just behind inadequate vision 28% and ahead of inadequate sponsor support 25%).

**Insolvency cascade (ties to C3).** Same UK construction ranking as §3. Legal/market notes (Freeths, Herrington Carmichael, Gardiner & Theobald): main-contractor insolvency halts the job and stresses every tier below. Mechanism, not a new statistic.

**KPMG** *Renewed Urgency on Third Party Risk*: you cannot outsource the risk; third-party failure becomes first-party damage. Qualitative only.

**Caveat.** IBM is cyber, not all cross-corporate risk. Pair it with PMI 27% if we want a project-management number rather than a CISO number.

## 7. People development

**Why it matters.** The people who do the work are a delivery variable. Burning a crew and bidding the next job with a new one is a client risk, not just an HR story.

**Headline.** Construction Industry Institute **RT-231**, *Construction Industry Craft Training in the United States and Canada*: on a single capital construction project, **each $1 invested in craft training can yield $1.30 to $3.00** in benefits (productivity, less turnover, absenteeism, rework). CII also: investing **1%** of the labour/wage budget on training paid off on a 24-month capital project and on ongoing maintenance/small-capital work.

- https://www.construction-institute.org/construction-industry-craft-training-in-the-united-states-and-canada
- Detailed report (CURT copy): http://curt.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/RT-231-1-Value-of-Craft-Training-Research-Detailed-Report.pdf

**Restated splits** (CII + NCCER / ENR): **+11%** productivity, **+14%** retention, **−26%** recordable injuries, **−23%** rework; up to **3:1** ROI.

- ENR: https://www.enr.com/articles/58709-a-compelling-case-for-construction-craft-training-the-return-on-investment-is-real
- NCCER: https://www.nccer.org/roi/

**Newer.** NCCER *From Training to Performance* (2025): 1,400+ craftworkers, ~200 employers; **84%** report training improved retention; productivity targets met **65%** of the time with training vs **52%** without.

**Caveat.** Strongest evidence is **construction craft**, not generic "L&D as % of profit." PBF's 0 / 0–5 / 5%+ banding is **internal scoring only**. Public question: did they invest in the people who did the work, and did it show up in delivery?

## 8. Team & business acumen (integrity and conduct)

**Why it matters.** Three layers: (a) conduct — whether last quarter's greens were true; (b) leadership across two organisations — silence, claims-as-ambush, communication that never crossed the wall; (c) **initiative** — bringing the client better ways of doing it and engaging on solutions, rather than executing the ticket. The framework wording ("leadership and interpersonal effectiveness across organisations", "balancing customer satisfaction with profitability") covers (c) and it is the half people forget.

**On (c), the initiative half.** No dedicated statistic needed — it is a behaviour, not a benchmark. If a number is wanted, the supplier-innovation evidence sits in §9; keep it there so C8 and C9 don't collapse into each other. C8 = what they did *with us*. C9 = whether the practice is worth copying *beyond us*.

**Parked for this post.** The TI bribery ranking below is accurate and quotable, but in the draft it dragged the paragraph towards anti-corruption and crowded out candour + initiative, which are the two behaviours that actually decide keep-or-drop. Hold it for a dedicated integrity post.

**Headline — sector integrity.** Transparency International **Bribe Payers Index 2011**: of 19 business sectors, **public works contracts and construction ranked last** (most bribery-prone), as it did in 2008. High-value investment + heavy government interaction. TI has not published a later BPI (they shifted to CPI). Still the last comparative sector ranking.

- https://www.files.ethz.ch/isn/134888/Bribe%20Payers%20Index%202011_single%20pages.pdf
- Earlier: GCR 2005 (construction focus); Stansbury on why the sector is slanted (uniqueness, permits, quality covered over).

**Order of magnitude, not a point estimate.** World Economic Forum (2016), citing CoST: losses through corruption "tend to range between **10 and 30%**"; CoST experience suggests a similar amount lost through mismanagement and inefficiency. OECD background paper repeats CoST 2012: **10–30%** of publicly funded construction investment. **Too wide to quote as a fact in the post.** Use: *the sector has spent decades at the bottom of the bribery rankings for a reason*.

- https://www.weforum.org/stories/2016/02/why-is-the-construction-industry-so-corrupt-and-what-can-we-do-about-it/

OECD *Foreign Bribery Report*: construction **15%** of foreign-bribery cases (extractives 19%, transport 15%). Sector concentration, not "15% of project cost."

**Headline — conduct / communication (the silent-green problem).** PMI *The Essential Role of Communications* (Pulse in-depth): of US$135 million at risk per US$1 billion spent, **56% (US$75 million)** is at risk due to **ineffective communications**. One-half of unsuccessful projects related to ineffective communications. Organisations that are minimally effective communicators report significantly fewer projects on goals, time, and budget.

- https://www.pmi.org/-/media/pmi/documents/public/pdf/learning/thought-leadership/pulse/the-essential-role-of-communications.pdf

Pulse 2021 appendix: **inadequate/poor communication** named by **28%** as a primary cause of project failure.

**Caveat.** Don't lecture anti-corruption. Public line: the dashboard can be green because nobody raised the amber. Integrity is whether those greens were true.

## 9. Innovation & industry advancement

**Why it matters.** The keep-or-drop question is partly "will they make the *next* job better?" Replicable practice, knowledge sharing, case-study worth. Stage 2 judging focuses strongly here (internal).

**Headline.** McKinsey, *Procuring innovation, wherever it may be*: **more than half** of innovations adopted today originate outside the organisation (suppliers, research centres, customers). **Externally sourced innovations are typically commercialised 40% faster** than home-grown ideas, often because they are already partly validated. Across sectors they estimate **25–45% of revenues** come from innovative products.

- https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/operations/our-insights/procuring-innovation-wherever-it-may-be
- Same 40% figure: *Managing your external supply system for innovation*

**Demand-side.** Gartner survey of **100** global procurement leaders: **89%** under pressure to deepen supplier relationships because of supply shortages (80% COVID exposure, 74% logistics, 63% inflation). SRM "Innovation Champions": steering committees **+59%** and supplier innovation events **+39%** more supplier innovation when used consistently. Today's SRM teams "fail to capture the majority of potential supplier innovation value."

- https://www.gartner.com/en/supply-chain/topics/supplier-relationship-management

**Academic.** Wang et al., *Journal of Innovation & Knowledge* (2023, n = 417): knowledge sharing **mediates** how supply-chain partnership drives innovation performance. DOI: 10.1016/j.jik.2023.100431

**Caveat.** None of this says "winners score 4–5 on C9." Public line stays: would we tell this story to someone else / is there something stealable.
