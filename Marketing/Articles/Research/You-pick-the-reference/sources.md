# Sources — "You pick the reference"

Fact-checked 2 Oct 2026. The post uses **one finding, two halves**. Split them and the line lies.

## The finding

TrustRadius, *Bridging the Trust Gap: B2B Tech Buying in the Age of AI* (ninth annual buyer report). Online survey, January 2025, of people involved in a software or hardware purchase in the past year. **2,058 technology buyers** and 490 technology vendors. Nominal incentive, a $10 gift card. Press release 8 Apr 2025.

- Report PDF: https://go.trustradius.com/rs/827-FOI-687/images/TrustRadius-Bridging-the-Trust-Gap-B2B-Tech-Buying-in-the-Age-of-AI.pdf
- Vendor write-up: https://www.trustradius.com/blog/bridging-the-trust-gap-b2b-tech-buying-in-the-age-of-ai
- Press release: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/bridging-the-trust-gaptrustradius-releases-its-ninth-annual-buyer-research-report-302422237.html

**The pair.**

- **54 percent** of buyers had a conversation with a user before purchasing.
- **23 percent** spoke with a vendor-supplied reference. The rest of those peer conversations went through professional networks, coworkers, and forums.

Vendors in the same study estimated the user-conversation figure at **38 percent**. Held back. A third number crowds the pair.

**Wording that is safe.** "TrustRadius asked technology buyers who they actually called. Fifty-four percent had spoken to a user. Twenty-three percent had spoken to the reference the vendor supplied."

**Say it is TrustRadius, and that the buyers were buying technology.** They sell reviews. The sample is not project contractors. Do not write "23 percent of clients" or "buyers do not check references."

## Not the September number

The 4 Sep post used an older TrustRadius finding, about one in seven buyers still consulting vendor-written materials. This is a later report and a different question: who they called, not whether they read the brochure. If a comment treats them as the same stat, they are not. Do not restate the one-in-seven figure here.

## Award facts used in the post

Already public in the certificate short and the 4 Sep draft: the client rates the project through a link the contractor never sees and cannot fill in; at least two professionals read the file and they do not work for you. "I help run it" is the disclaimer.

The film's third line, "a rating you did not coach," is that client link. It is not a published scoring formula.
