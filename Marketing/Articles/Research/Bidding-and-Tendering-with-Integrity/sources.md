# Sources: "Bidding and tendering with integrity"

Fact-checked 14 Aug 2026. Same rule as the rest: no number in the post unless it is in this file.

Most of this is **primary UK government material**, which is a step up in quality from the tender-rejection sources looked at for #8. It is also, unavoidably, UK-specific. Acknowledge that in the post rather than hiding it.

## 1. Late payment: the baseline

Large construction businesses paid **14%** of invoices late in 2025, i.e. **one in seven**, with a median of **33 days** to pay, unchanged from 2024. Department for Business and Trade payment-practices data, reported by BCIS.

- https://www.bcis.co.uk/news/large-construction-firms-paid-one-in-seven-invoices-late-in-2025/
- Do **not** convert 33 days to "days late". Median days-to-pay and days-beyond-terms are different measures.

Wales notes traditional sub-contract terms commonly run **60-90 days** (see PBA sources below), which is the contrast the post needs.

## 2. Retentions: the strongest material in this post

**What a retention is.** A sum withheld from payments to incentivise return to fix defects. Standard practice, not an abuse in itself. The question is where the money sits and whether it comes back.

**Where the money sits.** Pye Tait research for BEIS, *Retentions in the Construction Industry* (BEIS Research Paper 17, 2017): the majority of those holding retentions, clients and contractors alike, hold them in **a main bank account**. The report's own conclusion: *"for contractors there is no protection from upstream insolvencies, as retention monies held against their work are not typically ring-fenced, for example in a separate account."*

- https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a821ff740f0b62305b929d8/Retention_Payments_Pye_Tait_report.pdf
- **This is the line the post turns on.** The money owed to a subcontractor is not being kept safe. It is working capital.

**How often it is lost.** **44%** of surveyed contractors who had retentions held from them in the previous three years had experienced non-payment of retention monies due to **upstream insolvency** in that period. **But** it occurred on only **about 1% of all their contracts**, and the **median amount lost per affected contractor over three years was GBP 10,000**.

- **Mandatory honesty pairing.** 44% alone is misleading. Always give the 1% of contracts alongside it. The true shape is "common experience, rare event, aggregate cost that is still large".

**The aggregate.** The Government's own Impact Assessment estimated total retentions unpaid each year due to upstream insolvencies at **GBP 229 million** (2015 prices).

- https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a82b647e5274a2e8ab58f4b/Retentions_Consultation_Impact_Assessment_FINAL.pdf
- Consultation document: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/654258/2017.10.23_Retentions_Payments_Consultation_FINAL.pdf

**Also reported, use with care.** Around **71%** of contractors with retentions held had experienced **delays** in receiving them (Pye Tait, quoted by the Confederation of Construction Specialists). Delay, not loss. Weaker sourcing than the figures above because it comes via a trade body.

**Circulating version to handle carefully.** The widely quoted **"GBP 700 million of retentions lost over three years"** traces to trade bodies citing BEIS Research Paper 17, and is consistent with the official GBP 229m per year (229 x 3 = 687). **Prefer the official annual figure.** The Specialist Engineering Contractors' Group estimate of **GBP 7.8 billion of retentions unpaid over three years** is advocacy, on a different definition, and should not be used.

## 3. Insolvency: where it lands

Construction had the most company insolvencies of any industry in England and Wales: **3,950**, **17%** of cases with industry captured, in the **12 months to November 2025**.

- https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/company-insolvencies-december-2025/commentary-company-insolvency-statistics-december-2025
- Say **12 months to November 2025**, not "year to December 2025".

**Client-side consequence.** Rabbet, *Construction Payments Report* (2024): if a contractor fails mid-project, the client pays for the halt plus the replacement. Mechanism, not a statistic.

## 4. Why lowball bidding shows up as late payment down the chain

Petter Matthews (Executive Director, CoST International Secretariat) on weforum.org, Feb 2016, on why the UK moved away from lowest-price tendering over 10-15 years: it was not delivering cost savings, and *"lowest price bidding encourages contractors to bid for work at unrealistically low levels. It then becomes difficult for them to maintain standards and make profits, the quality of their work falls and they become more likely to make unjustified claims, delay payments to subcontractors and indulge in corrupt behaviour to reduce their losses."*

- https://www.weforum.org/stories/2016/02/why-is-the-construction-industry-so-corrupt-and-what-can-we-do-about-it/
- **Attribution care:** signed contributor piece; WEF's footer disclaims it as the author's own view. Say "the UK public sector moved away from lowest-price tendering because it was not saving money", never "the WEF found".
- **Scope discipline:** use this only for the clause that names **delayed payment to subcontractors**. The whole-life-cost argument belongs to Oliver's 12 Oct post.

## 5. Project bank accounts: the stealable practice

**What they are.** A ring-fenced bank account with **trust status**. The client deposits the certified interim amount, and named beneficiaries, meaning the main contractor plus tier 2 and tier 3 firms, are **paid simultaneously**. The main contractor still controls **how much** each firm is due, but **no longer exclusively controls when** they are paid. Crucially: *"A main contractor, whether solvent or insolvent, cannot withdraw money allocated to other supply chain beneficiaries from the PBA."*

- Scottish Government, SPPN 10/2016: https://www.gov.scot/publications/implementation-of-project-bank-accounts-in-construction-contracts-sppn-102016/
- Implementation guidance (Nov 2025 version): https://www.gov.scot/binaries/content/documents/govscot/publications/advice-and-guidance/2016/12/construction-projects-implementing-project-bank-accounts/documents/project-bank-accounts-implementation-in-construction-contracts-november-2025/project-bank-accounts-implementation-in-construction-contracts-november-2025/govscot%3Adocument/v14.0%2Bclean.pdf

**Speed.** Beneficiaries are paid **within five days** of the certified amount being deposited (Scotland). Wales puts it at **3-5 days**, against traditional sub-contract terms of **60-90 days**.

- Wales WPPN 011: https://www.gov.wales/wppn-011-project-bank-account-policy-guidelines-html

**Mandate.** Required on all Welsh Government construction and infrastructure projects that it funds in whole or part. Under part 3 of the **Social Partnership and Public Procurement (Wales) Act 2023**, coming into force in **2026**, public bodies with construction contracts over **GBP 2 million** must include PBA clauses or notify Welsh Ministers, with summaries of those notifications published.

**Savings claim, weaker.** "The government estimates that if fully implemented, project bank accounts can deliver almost 1% savings in the cost of construction projects" (Federation of Piling Specialists, citing a government estimate). Trade body relaying a figure. **Either skip it or attribute loosely.** The post does not need it; the mechanism is the point.
