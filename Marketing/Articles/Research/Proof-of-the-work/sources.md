# Sources — "Proof of the work"

Fact-checked 25 Sep 2026. The post uses **one** number. Everything else is background or a deliberate exclusion.

## The one number

**211,917 PMP certifications bestowed in 2024.** PMI Annual Report 2024, published figures page.

- https://www.pmi.org/-/media/pmi/documents/public/pdf/about/annual-reports/2024-annual-report.pdf

Why this one and not the stock total: it is exact, it is PMI's own reporting of its own year, and a single year is the more striking frame. It also cannot be read as an attack. PMI is reporting growth it is proud of.

**Wording that is safe.** "PMI handed out 211,917 new PMPs in 2024." Or rounded: "more than two hundred thousand people earned a PMP in a single year."

**Do not write** "211,917 people", strictly it is certifications bestowed. In practice these are new holders, but keep the noun PMI used.

## Context, not for the post

- **1.7 million+ PMP holders**, per PMI's PMP certification page. Useful if Peter wants the stock rather than the flow, but note the collision below before using both.
- **1,759,999 active certifications** across *all* PMI credentials, and **2.8 million** certified professionals since 1984 (Annual Report 2024). This is a different denominator from the PMP page's 1.7 million. Do not put the two 1.7s in the same sentence, they are measuring different things and someone in the comments will notice.
- **Late 2025 update:** over 1.85 million active certification holders as of end November 2025, +7% year on year (Pierre Le Manh, PMI CEO). Secondary source, all credentials, not PMP only. Fine as context, not as the post's number.
- **PMI's own framing of what the exam proves.** The certification handbook: the programme exists so holders "have demonstrated their knowledge through fair and valid measures". Useful if anyone argues in the comments that the letters already prove the work. https://www.pmi.org/-/media/pmi/documents/public/pdf/certifications/generic-certification-handbook.pdf

## Buyer-side evidence (held back)

Not needed at 200 words, and the 4 Sep post already spent the buyer-distrust card on TrustRadius. Kept here in case a longer version is ever commissioned.

- **UserEvidence, The Evidence Gap (2025),** n = 811 B2B software professionals: 78% cared most about proof of success with similar customers; 26% of deals failed for evidence-related reasons including unproven ROI and no live reference. B2B software, not contracting. https://userevidence.com/report/the-evidence-gap-2025/
- **UK government selection guidance (PPN 03/24, Annex A):** buyers may assess technical and professional ability through examples and references from past contracts, typically three, and references should be factual and verifiable with the named customer. Useful as "the buyer is already asking for this, formally." https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ppn-0324-standard-selection-questionnaire-sq/annex-a-guidance-on-the-selection-stage-process-including-standard-selection-questions-pcr-2015-html

## Hiring research (parked from the scrapped long draft)

Only if a long-form version is ever revived. Both papers agree years of experience is a weak predictor; they disagree on whether a work sample beats a knowledge test, so neither can be quoted alone.

- Schmidt and Hunter (1998), *Psychological Bulletin* 124(2), 262-274: job knowledge .48, work sample .54, job experience in years .18. DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.124.2.262
- Sackett, Zhang, Berry and Lievens (2022), *Journal of Applied Psychology* 107(11), 2040-2068: revised to job knowledge .40, work sample .33, years .07. DOI 10.1037/apl0000994
