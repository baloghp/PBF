## v1 (current), about 200 words

**Working title:** Proof of the work
*(alternatives: "211,917", "The letters are not the differentiator". Keep PCoTY out of the headline.)*

PMI handed out 211,917 new PMPs in 2024. In one year.

Good for the profession. Slightly awkward for your next bid, because the letters after your name prove that you know the work, and a few hundred thousand people a year can prove the same thing.

What none of them can copy is your work.

That is the part your capability statement is worst at. You wrote it yourself, so the buyer reads it as marketing, and they are not wrong.

Project Contractor of the Year exists to fix exactly that, and I help run it. You put one real project in. Two assessors who do not work for you read it, and they hold the same credentials you do. Your client rates the job through a link you never see and cannot fill in.

Title or no title, you walk away with something you could not have written: an account of your work that somebody else stands behind.

Your certificate says you know how. This says you did it.

Nominations close 31 October. ittd.space/pbf-pcoty

## Notes

- **Why it changed.** The earlier drafts argued the case properly and were, at 950 words with two meta-analyses, a reading assignment rather than a nudge. This one has one job: make a contractor curious enough to click. The hiring research is parked in `sources.md` if a long version is ever wanted.
- **One number, PMI's own.** 211,917 PMP certifications bestowed in 2024, from PMI's annual report. A single year is punchier than the 1.7 million stock figure, and it reads as growth rather than as a dig. Keep "handed out" or "earned"; do not add a second statistic.
- **The letters stay respected.** "Good for the profession", and the assessors hold the same credentials. That line does double duty: it keeps the tone friendly and it tells the reader the judging is not amateur. It also leaves Kris's 19 Oct PBP post intact.
- **Disclaimer is a clause,** "and I help run it", rather than the paragraph the long posts carry. At this length a full disclosure block would be a third of the post. "Organisers cannot enter" is dropped for space; add it back if the page reshares with a comment.
- **Process is three facts, no mechanics.** Two independent assessors, credentialed, client rates through a link the contractor cannot touch. No nine criteria, no scale, no weights.
- **"Your certificate says you know how. This says you did it."** is the line the post is built to reach. If anything gets cut for length, do not cut this.
- **No emoji.** Three hashtags. Hashtag call is still `#PCOTY2027`, same open question as the 4 Sep draft.
