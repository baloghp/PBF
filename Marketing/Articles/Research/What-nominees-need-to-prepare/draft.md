## v4 (current), about 720 words

**Working title:** Nobody is counting your words
*(alternatives: "What a nomination has to do", "The four jobs of a nomination". Keep PCoTY out of the headline.)*

Somebody asked me last week what it takes to put a nomination together. I said about two thousand words. That was the wrong answer, and not because the number is wrong.

Two thousand words is the ceiling on the main narrative, not the target. Same for the sections after it. Five hundred on what made the project exemplary, three hundred on impact, three hundred on lessons learned. Those are limits. Nobody scores a point for filling them.

So the more useful question is what the file has to do. Four things.

**It has to be checkable.** At least two assessors read every nomination independently, and for each criterion they have to write down the evidence they used. Which means a claim nobody can check is not evidence, it is marketing. Name the companies. Name the roles, the dates, the sequence. Give us a client contact who will actually answer. "We significantly improved delivery performance" survives exactly as long as it takes somebody to ask what it was compared to.

**It has to say what actually happened.** What the project was for. What the scope was. The milestones in order, and the events. Including the week it went sideways and what you did about it. A narrative with no events in it reads like a brochure, and a brochure gives an assessor nothing to write down. One of the sections also asks what value is still there six to twelve months after handover, which is not the same question as what you delivered.

**It has to feed all nine criteria.** I went through those last week: outcomes, agility, the commercial model, legal soundness, the interface, cross-corporate risk, people, conduct, and whether anyone should copy what you did. Every one of them gets scored separately. If nothing in your file speaks to how the money moved between the two companies, or to who decided what at the boundary, that is not a gap in your project. It is a gap in your file, and an assessor who finds nothing has to record that they found nothing. Silence is not neutral.

**It has to show you were exceptional, not competent.** Competent is the baseline, and the baseline does not win. The test I would apply before submitting: if this file could describe any well-run project in your sector, it is not finished.

Then there is the part that is not yours.

Your client representative gets an email from us with a link that only they receive. They rate you against seven criteria, sign, and agree that we can publish a short version of the story if you win. You will not see that link. You cannot forward it, fill it in, or upload it on their behalf.

Which puts two very small things on your critical path.

The first is the email address, and it has to be right, because getting it wrong fails silently. The link goes nowhere, and nobody finds out, including you.

The second is the warning. An unannounced email asking somebody to rate a supplier through a link is indistinguishable from phishing, and their IT department will agree with me. So tell your client it is coming, who it is from, and roughly when. Pick someone who still works there and still answers email.

And it has to be filled in by the closing date, the same as everything else. Nominations close 31 October, and the client form closes with them.

Which means that deadline is not really yours. It belongs to somebody at another company who does not yet know they are on your critical path. Anyone who has tried to get a customer case study approved knows the shape of this. Casey Hibbard, who does it for a living, says she has seen sign-off take anywhere from an hour to a year. It depends whose desk it lands on, and none of those desks are yours.

You do not have to get it right first time. The platform checks completeness within about a day and flags whatever is missing. But that only helps while there is calendar left, for you and for your client. Early and rough beats late and polished, which is not how any of us were trained to treat a deadline.

And if it turns out you cannot answer the impact question about your best project of last year, that is worth finding out now. Not because a jury is asking. Because the next client who shortlists you will.

Nominations open 1 September and close 31 October, at ittd.space/pbf-pcoty. Start with the call to your client. The words can wait. They are the only part entirely under your control.
