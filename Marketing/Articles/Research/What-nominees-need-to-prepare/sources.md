# Sources: "What nominees need to prepare"

Fact-checked 14 Aug 2026. Same rule as the other articles: do not put a number in the post unless it is in this file.

**Headline finding, and it cuts against the obvious angle.** The instinctive framing for a "what to prepare" post is "most applications fail on paperwork." The best available data says that is false where the scheme is well run: Horizon Europe excludes about **3.65%** of proposals before evaluation, and **purely formal errors are under 1%**. So the post must not claim nominations get thrown out on formalities. The real cost is quieter: four of the sections ask what happened after handover, and most organisations never recorded it.

---

**Correction carried into v2:** the word counts below are **maximums, not targets**. There is no minimum length and no credit for filling the limit. What the file has to do is four things (verifiable, what happened, feeds all nine criteria, proves exceptional) plus supply a client contact who will complete the evaluation form.

## 1. What the submission actually requires (internal)

[Submission Package](../../../Confluence/Project-Contractor-Of-The-Year-Award/98598913%20-%20Submission%20Package.md), status **"Draft - Subject to change"**:

- Written submission **2,000 words max**: objectives, scope/baseline, outcomes vs baseline, interface and governance, commercial/contract model and liquidity, risk handling, integrity controls
- **"What makes this project exemplary?" 500 words**, required. Scored under Innovation and Industry Advancement
- **"Impact measurement and transformation" 300 words**, required. Quantifiable outcomes, transformation evidence, **what impact remains 6-12 months after completion**, ROI. Scored under Project success
- **"Lessons learned and industry contribution" 300 words**, required. What you learned, how you are sharing it, replicability
- **Five artifacts:** draft contract matrix; customer-contractor interface RACI; people-development percentage of net income/profit; code of conduct acknowledgment; integrity plan
- **Client Assessment Form:** client rep rates **7 criteria (0-10) with weights**, signs, and consents to (a) evaluation and communications and (b) short story publication if winner or top-3 finalist. Client uploads the signed PDF; nominee enters the total score
- **Publication consent** for a short case profile
- **Automated pre-screening** checks completeness, eligibility and attachments, **typically within 24 hours**. Gaps are flagged on the nominee dashboard and the Communications Coordinator notifies the nominee with a deadline to fix
- **No Nominee Coach this cycle** ([DR-002](../../../Confluence/Project-Contractor-Of-The-Year-Award/245989377%20-%20DR-002%20—%20Drop%20Nominee%20Coach%20role%20(Post-UAT).md)); support is the platform help channel

The four prose limits add up to 3,100 words, but **do not present that as a workload**. They are ceilings. A short, well-evidenced file beats a long one. Do **not** publish the 0-5 assessor scale, the criterion weights, or the 0% / 0-5% / 5%+ people-development bands.

## 1b. Why "checkable" is a real constraint, not advice

- **At least two assessors per nomination**, scoring independently, and each must **"provide per-criterion comments and evidence notes"**. They also **verify eligibility and completeness at assessment time**. Source: [Roles and RACI](../../../Confluence/Project-Contractor-Of-The-Year-Award/117964816%20-%20Roles%20and%20RACI.md).
- Discrepancies more than 2 points from the median are **moderated by the PM**, so an outlier score gets argued over rather than averaged away quietly.
- The framework's lowest descriptor is literally **"not demonstrated / missing evidence"**, i.e. an unevidenced criterion is not skipped, it is marked. **Say this as "silence is not neutral"**, never by quoting the scale.
- **Stage 2 is a 20-minute presentation plus 15 minutes of Q&A** before a live panel ([2.0 Assessment](../../../Confluence/Project-Contractor-Of-The-Year-Award/129204226%20-%202.0%20Assessment.md)). This is the single best argument for writing only what you can defend. **Reserved for the 14 Sep post** so it is not spent here.

## 1c. The four jobs, mapped to what the platform asks for

## 2. The record-keeping problem (researched, then cut from the post)

> **Not used in v4.** This was the spine of v1 and a single paragraph in v3. Cut because the post's argument is now mechanical, and three statistics were carrying one point. Kept in full because it is verified, and because the **17%** benefits-realisation figure is the designated fallback if the post ever reads thin: use it alone, next to the "6-12 months after handover" clause. Also a ready-made spine for a future post on why nobody can evidence their own best project.

**Lessons are captured once, then lost.** PMI conference paper, *Business Benefits: the value of lessons learned* (n = 45 project managers, public and private sector): **89%** of lessons learned are captured **at the end** of projects, and only **31%** of respondents said their organisation has a database or system to store them for future use. About **87%** did lessons-learned activities only in a post-project review. A separate study cited there found **44%** had some way to apply lessons in planning future projects.

- https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/business-benefits-value-lessons-learned-7116
- **Caveat: n = 45.** Small. Use as "the pattern practitioners report", not "research proves". Directionally consistent with the Pulse below.

**Capturing is common, using it is not.** PMI *Pulse of the Profession: Capturing the Value of Project Management Through Knowledge Transfer*: **81%** of organisations capture knowledge via lessons learned and debriefings, but only **39%** apply that knowledge and only **27%** assess the benefits of doing so. Organisations most effective at knowledge transfer improve project outcomes by nearly **35%**, and are three times as likely to have a formal process (**92%** vs **33%**).

- https://www.pmi.org/-/media/pmi/documents/public/pdf/learning/thought-leadership/pulse/capture-value-knowledge-transfer.pdf
- Best single pairing for the post: **81% write them down, 39% ever use them.**

**Nobody measures what happened after handover.** PMI *Sustain Benefits to Optimize Business Value*: only **17%** of organisations report high benefits-realisation maturity; high-maturity organisations see **50% more** projects meeting original goals. Carried over from the [winning-nomination sources](../What-makes-a-winning-nomination/sources.md), where it was verified on 14 Aug.

- Directly relevant: the form asks what impact remains **6-12 months** after completion. For most nominators that number does not exist yet, which is the honest reason to start early.

## 2b. How the client form actually works, and why the docs are wrong

**Confirmed by Peter, 14 Aug 2026 (authoritative, overrides the Confluence pages below):**

- **We email the client representative** a **unique link** to the evaluation form
- **Nobody else receives or knows that link.** The nominee cannot see it, forward it, complete it, or upload the result on the client's behalf
- Therefore the nominee's job is to **supply the correct contact email** and to **make sure the client responds**
- **The form must be completed by the nomination closing date**, 31 October 2026. It is not a post-close item

**Three consequences the post uses:**

1. **A wrong email address fails silently.** The link goes to nobody, and no one is alerted, including the nominee. This is the only single-field typo in the whole submission that can quietly sink it.
2. **An unannounced request to rate a supplier via a link looks like phishing.** Spam quarantine or a cautious client is a realistic failure mode, so the nominee must warn them in advance: who it is from, roughly when it arrives.
3. **The deadline is not the nominee's.** It belongs to somebody at another company who does not know they are on the critical path. This is the mechanical reason "start with the client call" beats "start writing".

**Documentation conflict, needs fixing upstream.** Four pages still describe a **nominee-driven** flow, which contradicts the above:

| --- | --- |
| [Submission Package](../../../Confluence/Project-Contractor-Of-The-Year-Award/98598913%20-%20Submission%20Package.md) | *"You will provide a QR code or link to your client representative"*; *"Client uploads signed PDF"*; *"You enter the total score"* |
| [1.0 Submission and Intake](../../../Confluence/Project-Contractor-Of-The-Year-Award/130514945%20-%201.0%20Submission%20and%20Intake.md) | *"Obtain Client Assessment Form (client completes, signs, uploads; nominee enters totals)"* |
| [Roles and RACI](../../../Confluence/Project-Contractor-Of-The-Year-Award/117964816%20-%20Roles%20and%20RACI.md) | Nominee task: *"Obtain Client Assessment Form"* |

## 3. The client signature is the long pole

**Practitioner evidence, not research.** Casey Hibbard, author of *Stories That Sell* and a customer-story specialist, quoted widely on approval timelines: **"I've seen story signoff take anywhere from an hour to a year."** Attribute as experience. Do not dress it as a statistic.

- https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/6-simple-steps-gaining-final-customer-approval-case-study-john-cole (quotes Hibbard)

**Supporting, weaker.** Agency sources put a written B2B case study at **six to twelve weeks** once customer review, legal sign-off and quote approval are counted. Multiple independent practitioner sources agree on the direction; none is research. Safe use: "anyone who has tried to get a customer case study approved knows the shape of this." Do not quote 6-12 weeks as a fact.

**Why it bites here.** Our ask is heavier than a testimonial: the client rates seven criteria, signs, and consents to publication. That routes through the client's legal or comms function in exactly the way a case study does. Also relevant: in regulated sectors, legal frequently blocks named attribution, which is an argument for raising it early rather than at the deadline.

## 4. Administrative failure: the honest counterweight

**Use this to avoid overclaiming.** Analysis of eCORDA data (cut-off May 2025) on Horizon Europe proposals that never reach evaluation: aggregate exclusion **3.65%**, of which **ineligibility is about two-thirds**, and **purely formal errors stay under 1%**. Consortium actions fail at **5.8%** vs **1.2%** for single-beneficiary schemes, i.e. the more parties involved, the more administrative risk. That last point is genuinely useful for us, because a nomination involves at least two organisations.

- https://www.horizontevropa.cz/en/news/yiifnews/3489
- EC rules on what "admissible" means (complete form, all annexes, before deadline): https://webgate.ec.europa.eu/funding-tenders-opportunities/spaces/OM/pages/1867931/Admissibility+and+eligibility+check

**Real but not generalisable.** Central Procurement Board of Namibia, March 2025: chairperson Mary Shiimi said **more than 50%** of submitted bids are disqualified for non-compliance, citing incomplete forms, incorrectly completed forms and late submission. A named official on the record, so quotable, but one country's public procurement. Do not present it as an industry norm.

- https://www.namibian.com.na/over-50-of-submitted-bids-disqualified-by-procurement-board/

## 5. Unresolved tension: "name it" vs "minimise personal data"

The post tells nominators to name companies, roles, dates and a client contact, because an unverifiable claim cannot be scored. The [Submission Package](../../../Confluence/Project-Contractor-Of-The-Year-Award/98598913%20-%20Submission%20Package.md) tells them the opposite in places: *"minimize personal data"*, *"use job titles/roles instead of names where possible"*, *"the client's technical lead" rather than specific names*.

Both are right, at different levels, and the post must not send people into a warning screen:

- **Organisations should be named.** GDPR covers personal data; the package says explicitly that company and project data is not in scope. So company names, contract structure, and project facts are the verifiable layer.
- **Individuals should be roles**, except where a named person is functionally required. The client representative is exactly that exception: they sign, they consent, they can be contacted. The package already assumes a named, reachable client rep.
- **Safe formulation for the post:** name the *organisations*, describe people by *role*, and supply one real client contact. Avoid "name the stakeholders", which reads as "list individuals".

**Action:** confirm this reading with whoever owns the GDPR wording before 26 Aug. If the platform's guidance stays as-is, the post's "name the roles, the dates, the sequence" line is compliant, but "name the companies" needs to stay at organisation level.
