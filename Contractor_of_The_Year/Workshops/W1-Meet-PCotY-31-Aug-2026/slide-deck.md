## Slide 3 — The observation

**Title:** The contractor is usually a footnote

**On slide:**

- We celebrate the project. We name the customer. We remember the outcome.
- The firm that actually delivered the contracted work is in the acknowledgements.
- That is not a complaint about project managers. It is a hole in how the profession keeps score.

**Speaker notes:**

Start here, not with the award brand.

Most of the awards in our field sit on the customer side: the PM, the PMO, the internal programme. Fair enough. That is one half of a contracted project.

The other half is the independent contractor: the people who took the risk, ran the interface, and still had to get paid. When the work is good, the story still tends to be told as if the customer did it alone.

Project Business is the name for that other half. Customer and contractor, under contract, across a corporate boundary. That is the field this award is in.

**Time box:** 4 minutes.

## Slide 4 — Why the award exists

**Title:** Why we built an award on that hole

**On slide:**

- To name outstanding contracted delivery, not just a finished job
- To put customer and contractor on the same page, with evidence from both
- To leave a short case profile others can actually learn from
- First cycle. We are setting the standard in public, not polishing a tenth edition.

**Speaker notes:**

The award is not a vendor beauty parade and not a popularity vote. It is not "lowest price, on time, please clap."

It exists because the quiet judgement calls in contracted work almost never make the case study: a fair price, a risk raised early, a supply chain that actually got paid, an interface that worked when the spec did not.

We want those stories named, scored against published criteria, and — for winners and top finalists — written up so the rest of the field can steal from them.

First cycle means the volume is unknown. That is fine. Quality of the standard matters more than filling a room.

**Time box:** 4 minutes.

## Slide 5 — Competence is not the prize

**Title:** Beyond competence

**On slide:**


| Competent                         | What we are looking for                         |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Did the job well                  | Did something uniquely useful                   |
| Had good processes                | Left a measurable change after handover         |
| The customer was satisfied        | The profession can learn from it                |
| Followed best practice            | Created a practice worth copying                |
| Finished the project              | Changed how the two organisations work together |


**Speaker notes:**

This is the distinction the whole cycle hangs on.

A nomination that says "we delivered on time and the client was happy" is a complete project report. It is not yet an award nomination. That bar is necessary. It is not sufficient.

Ask the room, if they will type in chat: think of a contractor you would put forward. Was it because the dashboard was green, or because of a moment when something was going wrong — or unexpectedly right?

Do not collect the stories now. The question is to plant the name.

**Time box:** 4 minutes.

## Slide 6 — The last question

**On slide:**

- Competent work with nothing others can use will not win, even with high delivery scores
- Criterion 9 — Innovation and Industry Advancement — is where this lives

**Speaker notes:**

If you remember one thing from the "why": we are not looking for the cleanest project. We are looking for the one we would be willing to put our name on as a published example.

## Slide 7 — Who can be nominated

**Title:** Eligibility, in plain terms

**On slide:**

- A cross-corporate customer-contractor project. Any industry.
- You acted as an independent contractor for a paying client, under contract.
- Completed in 2024-2025, or still running at nomination.
- The client representative must complete their own evaluation. No form, no nomination.
- Winners and top-3 finalists accept a short case profile being published.

**Speaker notes:**

Not internal projects. Not "we did a great job for our own PMO." The boundary has to be real: two organisations, a contract, money.

Any industry. We are not a construction award that politely invites others. Software, health, energy, professional services — if it is contracted delivery, it is in scope.

Still-running projects are allowed. The impact section then has to show what is already there, not a promise.

The client form is the gate people underestimate. Come back to that on slide 10.

**Time box:** 4 minutes.

## Slide 9 — What a nomination actually is

**Title:** The packet

**On slide:**

Four written pieces (ceilings, not targets):

1. Project narrative — up to 2,000 words
2. What makes this exemplary — up to 500
3. Impact and transformation — up to 300
4. Lessons learned — up to 300

Plus evidence: contract matrix, interface RACI, people-development figure, code of conduct, integrity plan.

**Speaker notes:**

Nobody scores extra for filling the word count. Empty pages padded with process language are worse than a short, evidenced story.

The "exemplary" section is the one that feeds Criterion 9. If it could describe any well-run project in the sector, it is not finished.

Artifacts are there to make the narrative checkable. A RACI that only existed on a kickoff slide will read as a RACI that only existed on a kickoff slide.

Point at the Nominee Guide. Do not walk the evidence list line by line. W2 in September is the nomination clinic.

**Time box:** 4 minutes.

## Slide 10 — The client is not a reference

**Title:** Two actors, or it does not count

**On slide:**

- We email the client a unique link. The nominee cannot fill it, forward it, or upload it.
- Seven operational ratings, 0-10, with weights. Evidence notes on high and low scores.
- Due 31 October, same close as the nomination.
- A wrong work email fails silently. Warn them the message is coming, and that it is not phishing.

**Speaker notes:**

This is the two-actor rule. Contractor story plus client assessment. One without the other is incomplete.

Assessors score nine award criteria. The client scores a different sheet: communication, team, safety communication, documentation, change, management attention, execution. Do not mix the two scales in your head, and do not tell nominees to "get a nice letter."

Practical advice, because this is where nominations die: pick someone who still works there and still answers email. Tell them this week that a form is coming in September. The words can wait. The client relationship cannot.

**Time box:** 5 minutes. This is the nomination-risk slide. Slow down.

## Slide 12 — Two stages

**Title:** How a nomination is judged

**On slide:**

1. **Intake.** Eligibility and completeness. Does not score quality.
2. **Stage 1.** Independent assessors. Nine criteria, 1-10. At least two people per nomination.
3. **Stage 2.** Shortlist presents to a judging panel. 20 minutes plus 15 minutes of questions. Panel rescores the same nine.

No volunteer coach. Questions go to program support. Status lives on the dashboard.

**Speaker notes:**

Intake is a gate, not a jury. Missing files get flagged. Quality is a human score.

Stage 1 is remote, written, independent. Assessors do not talk the nominee through the packet.

Stage 2 is live. Same framework, new scores, plus what the room hears in the Q&A. Oliver holds a deciding vote only if the panel is stuck.

If you are here as a possible assessor, you are Stage 1. You are not automatically a judge.

**Time box:** 4 minutes.

## Slide 13 — The nine criteria

**Title:** What Stage 1 and Stage 2 both score

**On slide:**

1. Project success — did they deliver, and is anything still there?
2. Agility and adaptability — change without chaos
3. Commercial model — was the price real, and did the chain get paid?
4. Legal soundness — complete, enforceable, fitted to how they actually worked
5. Interface and governance — the customer-contractor boundary
6. Risk management — the risks that sit between the two companies
7. People development — investment in the people who did the work
8. Team and business acumen — candour, initiative, integrity
9. Innovation and industry advancement — would we publish this?

**Speaker notes:**

Do not teach all nine. Read them once, slowly.

The point for nominees: if your packet only answers 1, you have a project report.

The point for assessors: you will score all nine, with a comment on each. Silence in the packet is not a 5. If it is not there, say so.

Full descriptors live in the Assessment Guide. Calibration comes later for people who take a seat.

**Time box:** 5 minutes.

## Slide 14 — Scoring, in one slide

**Title:** 1 to 10, same scale for assessors and judges

**On slide:**

- 1 = not demonstrated
- 5-6 = solid professional work
- 9-10 = exceptional and evidenced
- Nine criteria, 90 is the maximum from one reader
- Two independent reviews (three when we can). Difference of more than 4 points on a criterion gets moderated.
- Do not save 10 for "someone else." If the evidence is there, give it.

**Speaker notes:**

Client sheet is 0-10 with weights. Award criteria are 1-10. Different instruments. Do not convert.

1 is not a moral judgement. It means the packet did not show it.

This is enough scoring theory for today. Assessors will see it again in calibration.

**Time box:** 2 minutes.

## Slide 15 — Stage 1, if you assess

**Title:** What an assessor actually does

**On slide:**

- Confirm no conflict of interest. If there is one, stop. We reassign.
- Read the packet, including the client evaluation if it is shown to you.
- Score 1-9, comment on each, submit by the deadline.
- 3-5 nominations. Remote. Your own schedule.
- You do not chase missing files, coach nominees, or share scores with them.

**Speaker notes:**

Intake already checked completeness. You are scoring quality.

If a required artifact is genuinely absent, that is evidence for the related criterion, not a reason to reopen intake.

You may be asked into a short moderation call if two readers diverge. Discuss evidence, not ego.

**Time box:** 3 minutes.

**On slide:**

**Speaker notes:**

Short. Most people on this call will never present here. Nominees need to know it exists so they do not treat Stage 1 as the whole award.

## Slide 17 — Why become an assessor

**Title:** Somebody has to decide what outstanding means

**On slide:**

- You will read real nominations from real contracts
- Delivery story, commercial model, governance, the client's own assessment
- Scored against published criteria, next to peers who do this work
- The interesting part is rarely the slider. It is seeing how strong operators structure and defend their work.

**Speaker notes:**

This is the pitch, not a job description. Match the EOI page. Do not oversell.

We need people who already know contracted delivery. That is why the gate is PMP or PBP.

If you want to see how the best packets in the field are built, this is the way in. Scoring is the mechanism. The exposure is the value.

**On slide:**

**Speaker notes:**

No "good standing" language. PBP has no such status. PMP or PBP is the gate. Strong experience exceptions exist, but do not advertise a back door on this call.
