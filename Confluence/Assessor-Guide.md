# Assessor Guide (Stage 1)

**Parent page:** [Roles and RACI](https://ittd.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/PBF/pages/117964816)

---

## Overview

Assessors are volunteers who conduct Stage 1 screening and evaluation of nominations. You will independently review and score nominations using the Evaluation Framework, and may also help prepare nominees for Stage 2 presentations if requested.

---

## Key Responsibilities

### Stage 1 Evaluation (2.1)

* Review assigned nominations
* Check for COI and self-report immediately if identified
* Verify eligibility and completeness
* Score using Evaluation Framework (0-5 scale)
* Provide per-criterion comments and evidence notes
* Complete assessments within SLA

### Optional: Stage 2 Preparation Support

* Help prepare nominees for Stage 2 presentations (if requested by Nominee Coach)
* Provide coaching and feedback to nominees

---

## Stage 1 Evaluation Process

### Step 1: Receive Assignment

**When you're assigned a nomination:**

1. **Review assignment notification:**
   - Note nominee category (Small, Medium, Large)
   - Note project type and industry
   - Note assessment deadline
   - Access nomination materials

2. **Confirm assignment:**
   - Acknowledge receipt of assignment
   - Confirm you can complete assessment by deadline
   - If you cannot, notify Nominee Coach immediately

3. **Access nomination materials:**
   - Written submission (≤2,000 words)
   - "What Makes This Exemplary?" section (500 words)
   - "Impact Measurement & Transformation" section (300 words)
   - "Lessons Learned & Industry Contribution" section (300 words)
   - Evidence artifacts (contract matrix, RACI, People Development metric, etc.)
   - Client Assessment Form

### Step 2: COI Check (IMMEDIATE - Before Review)

**Before reviewing the nomination, check for conflicts of interest:**

**What constitutes a COI:**
- Personal relationship with nominee or client
- Financial interest in the project or organization
- Current or recent business relationship
- Employment relationship (past or present)
- Family relationship
- Any situation that could affect your objectivity

**If COI identified:**
1. **STOP reviewing immediately**
2. **Notify Nominee Coach immediately** (within 24 hours)
3. **Document recusal:**
   - State reason for recusal
   - Do not review or score the nomination
   - Return materials to Nominee Coach

**If no COI:**
- Proceed with evaluation
- Document that COI check was completed

### Step 3: Eligibility and Completeness Verification

**Verify the following before scoring:**

**Eligibility:**
- [ ] Project type: Cross-corporate customer-contractor project
- [ ] Independent contractor for paying client under contract
- [ ] Completion window: Completed within last 2 years (2024-2025) OR currently running
- [ ] Category classification correct (headcount-based: 1-25, 26-100, 101+)

**Completeness:**
- [ ] Written submission present (≤2,000 words)
- [ ] "What Makes This Exemplary?" section completed (500 words)
- [ ] "Impact Measurement & Transformation" section completed (300 words)
- [ ] "Lessons Learned & Industry Contribution" section completed (300 words)
- [ ] All evidence artifacts present
- [ ] Client Assessment Form completed and signed

**If incomplete or ineligible:**
- Document missing items or eligibility issues
- Notify Nominee Coach
- Do not proceed with scoring until complete

### Step 4: Initial Review

**Read through the entire nomination package:**

1. **Read written submission** to understand:
   - Project objectives and scope
   - Outcomes vs baseline
   - Interface & governance approach
   - Commercial/contract model
   - Risk handling
   - Integrity controls

2. **Review "What Makes This Exemplary?" section:**
   - What sets this apart?
   - Innovation & uniqueness
   - Broader impact
   - Industry contribution

3. **Review "Impact Measurement & Transformation" section:**
   - Quantifiable outcomes
   - Transformation evidence
   - Long-term benefits
   - ROI/value demonstration

4. **Review "Lessons Learned & Industry Contribution" section:**
   - Key learnings
   - Knowledge sharing activities
   - Industry contribution
   - Replicability

5. **Review evidence artifacts:**
   - Contract matrix
   - Customer-contractor interface RACI
   - People Development metric
   - Code of conduct acknowledgment
   - Integrity plan

6. **Review Client Assessment Form:**
   - Note scores and ratings
   - Review evidence notes
   - Consider client perspective

### Step 5: Scoring Using Evaluation Framework

**Score each criterion independently (0-5 scale):**

Use the following scoring approach:

**0 = Not demonstrated / missing evidence**
- Criterion not addressed or no evidence provided

**1 = Poor**
- Minimal evidence, significant gaps, below standard practice

**2 = Fair**
- Some evidence, minor improvements, meets basic requirements

**3 = Good**
- Solid evidence, good practices, meets expectations

**4 = Very Good**
- Strong evidence, excellent practices, exceeds expectations

**5 = Excellent**
- Outstanding evidence, exemplary practices, sets new standards

#### Criterion 1: Project Success (Outcomes)

**What to evaluate:**
- Delivery performance vs baseline (scope, time, cost, quality)
- Benefits realization
- Handover effectiveness
- **Measurable impact and transformation** (from "Impact Measurement & Transformation" section)

**Scoring guidance:**
- **0-1:** Significant variances, poor outcomes, no measurable impact
- **2:** Met baseline, some variances, limited measurable impact
- **3:** Met or exceeded baseline, good outcomes, some measurable impact
- **4:** Exceeded baseline, strong outcomes, clear measurable impact and transformation
- **5:** Outstanding performance, exceptional outcomes, significant measurable impact and lasting transformation

**Key questions:**
- Did they deliver on scope, time, cost, quality?
- What measurable impact did they create?
- How did the project transform the client's business?
- What long-term benefits remain?

#### Criterion 2: Agility & Adaptability

**What to evaluate:**
- Responsiveness to change
- Effective change control/requirements management
- Suitability to chosen contracting model

**For T&M contracts, also consider:**
- Billing frequency and cash flow alignment
- Rate transparency and justification
- Client cost visibility and control
- Effectiveness of flexible scope management
- Balance between adaptability and budget awareness

**Scoring guidance:**
- **0-1:** Rigid, poor change management, frequent conflicts
- **2:** Basic change management, some flexibility
- **3:** Good change management, responsive to changes
- **4:** Excellent change management, highly adaptable, effective processes
- **5:** Outstanding adaptability, innovative change approaches, seamless flexibility

**Key questions:**
- How did they handle changes?
- Were change processes effective?
- Did they balance flexibility with control?

#### Criterion 3: Commercial Model (Liquidity Impact)

**What to evaluate:**
- Price realism
- Milestone cash flow
- Advances/retentions
- Effect on both parties' liquidity and solvency

**Scoring guidance:**
- **0-1:** Unrealistic pricing, poor cash flow, liquidity issues
- **2:** Basic pricing, some cash flow considerations
- **3:** Realistic pricing, reasonable cash flow, fair terms
- **4:** Well-structured pricing, excellent cash flow management, balanced risk-sharing
- **5:** Outstanding commercial model, optimal liquidity for both parties, innovative approaches

**Key questions:**
- Was pricing realistic?
- Did cash flow support both parties?
- Were payment terms fair and balanced?

#### Criterion 4: Legal Soundness

**What to evaluate:**
- Applicable law and jurisdiction
- Warranty/liability limits
- Privity and completeness
- Suitability of contracting approach to collaboration model

**Scoring guidance:**
- **0-1:** Incomplete contract, missing key terms, unenforceable
- **2:** Basic contract, some gaps, mostly enforceable
- **3:** Complete contract, standard terms, enforceable
- **4:** Well-structured contract, comprehensive terms, well-suited to model
- **5:** Outstanding contract, innovative terms, perfectly suited to collaboration model

**Key questions:**
- Is the contract complete and enforceable?
- Are terms appropriate for the collaboration model?
- Are risks properly allocated?

#### Criterion 5: Interface and Governance

**What to evaluate:**
- Clarity of obligations at customer-contractor boundary
- Customer provisions/enabling services vs contractor deliverables
- Escalation paths
- Decision rights
- Cross-corporate governance structures

**Scoring guidance:**
- **0-1:** Unclear boundaries, poor governance, frequent conflicts
- **2:** Basic governance, some clarity, occasional issues
- **3:** Clear boundaries, good governance, effective processes
- **4:** Excellent governance, clear boundaries, well-defined processes
- **5:** Outstanding governance, innovative structures, seamless collaboration

**Key questions:**
- Were boundaries clear?
- Was governance effective?
- Were escalation paths clear and used appropriately?

#### Criterion 6: Risk Management (Cross-Corporate)

**What to evaluate:**
- Identification of cross-party risks:
  - Financial and commercial exposure
  - Operational and supply chain continuity
  - Information and IP protection
  - Cybersecurity and technology interconnectivity
  - Regulatory and compliance alignment
  - Reputational and governance consistency
  - Geopolitical and environmental resilience
- Mitigations and contingency plans

**Scoring guidance:**
- **0-1:** No risk identification, no mitigations, high exposure
- **2:** Basic risk identification, some mitigations
- **3:** Good risk identification, standard mitigations
- **4:** Comprehensive risk identification, strong mitigations, proactive management
- **5:** Outstanding risk management, innovative mitigations, comprehensive coverage

**Key questions:**
- Were cross-corporate risks identified?
- Were mitigations appropriate and effective?
- Was there contingency planning?

#### Criterion 7: People Development

**What to evaluate:**
- Investment in staff development as % of net income/profit
- Categories: 0%, 0-5%, or 5%+
- Impact on project delivery and customer satisfaction
- Evidence of training, certifications, knowledge sharing

**Scoring guidance:**
- **0:** 0% investment, no development activities
- **1:** 0% investment, minimal development
- **2:** 0-5% investment, some development activities
- **3:** 0-5% investment, good development program
- **4:** 5%+ investment, strong development program, clear impact
- **5:** 5%+ investment, outstanding development program, significant impact

**Key questions:**
- What % of profit was invested in people development?
- What development activities were conducted?
- What was the impact on project delivery?

#### Criterion 8: Team and Business Acumen (Integrity and Conduct)

**What to evaluate:**
- Leadership and interpersonal effectiveness
- Ability to balance customer satisfaction with profitability
- Profit-center management by contractor PMs
- Adherence to cross-corporate code of conduct
- Anti-corruption stance
- Transparency and documentation discipline

**Scoring guidance:**
- **0-1:** Poor leadership, conflicts, no integrity controls
- **2:** Basic leadership, some integrity controls
- **3:** Good leadership, standard integrity controls
- **4:** Excellent leadership, strong integrity controls, transparent
- **5:** Outstanding leadership, exemplary integrity, innovative approaches

**Key questions:**
- Was leadership effective?
- Were integrity controls in place and followed?
- Was there transparency and documentation?

#### Criterion 9: Innovation & Industry Advancement ⭐ (CRITICAL)

**This is the key differentiator for winners. Pay special attention.**

**What to evaluate:**
- **Innovation & Uniqueness** (from "What Makes This Exemplary?" section)
- **Industry Contribution** (from "Lessons Learned & Industry Contribution" section)
- **Broader Impact** (from "What Makes This Exemplary?" section)
- **Case Study Worthiness** - Would we want to publish this?

**Performance Descriptors:**
- **0:** Not demonstrated / missing evidence
- **1:** Poor – Standard approach, no innovation, no industry contribution
- **2:** Fair – Some novel elements, minor improvements, limited knowledge sharing
- **3:** Good – Solid innovation, some industry relevance, basic knowledge sharing
- **4:** Very Good – Significant innovation, industry-relevant, active knowledge sharing, replicable practices
- **5:** Excellent – Groundbreaking approach, advances the profession, extensive knowledge sharing, highly replicable, case study worthy

**Key questions:**
- What did they do differently?
- How does this advance the profession?
- Would we want to publish this as a case study?
- Is this truly exemplary, not just competent?

**Note:** Winners should typically score 4-5 on this criterion.

### Step 6: Provide Comments and Evidence Notes

**For each criterion, provide:**

1. **Score justification:**
   - Why this score?
   - What evidence supports it?
   - What strengths were observed?
   - What gaps or weaknesses were identified?

2. **Evidence notes:**
   - Reference specific sections of submission
   - Reference specific artifacts
   - Note any particularly strong or weak areas

3. **Constructive feedback:**
   - What worked well?
   - What could be improved?
   - Specific recommendations

**Example comment format:**
```
Criterion: Innovation & Industry Advancement
Score: 4

Justification: The project demonstrated significant innovation through [specific example]. The approach to [specific area] was novel and industry-relevant. Active knowledge sharing was evident through [publications/presentations]. The practices are replicable and would benefit others.

Strengths:
- [Specific strength 1]
- [Specific strength 2]

Areas for improvement:
- [Specific area 1]
- [Specific area 2]

Evidence: Referenced in "What Makes This Exemplary?" section, paragraph 3; "Lessons Learned" section, paragraph 2.
```

### Step 7: Submit Assessment

**Before submitting:**

1. **Review your scores:**
   - Ensure all criteria scored (0-5)
   - Verify scores are consistent with comments
   - Check that Innovation & Industry Advancement received appropriate attention

2. **Review comments:**
   - Ensure comments are constructive
   - Verify evidence is referenced
   - Check for clarity and completeness

3. **Submit assessment:**
   - Submit by deadline
   - Confirm submission received
   - Notify Nominee Coach if you need extension

**After submission:**
- You may be contacted for moderation if scores differ significantly from other assessors
- Be available to discuss your assessment if needed

## Optional: Stage 2 Preparation Support

**If requested by Nominee Coach, you may help prepare nominees for Stage 2 presentations:**

### When Requested

1. **Nominee Coach will contact you:**
   - Ask if you're available to help
   - Provide nominee contact information
   - Share presentation requirements

2. **Coordinate with nominee:**
   - Schedule preparation session(s)
   - Review presentation draft
   - Provide feedback and coaching

### How to Help

**Focus areas:**
- **What makes them exemplary?** - Help them articulate this clearly
- **Measurable impact** - Ensure they can demonstrate transformation
- **Industry contribution** - Help them explain knowledge sharing
- **Presentation structure** - Ensure all required areas covered
- **Q&A preparation** - Anticipate likely questions

**Guidelines:**
- Provide constructive feedback
- Focus on strengths and how to highlight them
- Help identify areas that need clarification
- Do not write the presentation for them
- Maintain objectivity (you're helping, not advocating)

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't share scores or assessment details
- Don't promise outcomes
- Don't advocate for the nominee
- Don't provide information about other nominees

## Common Issues & Solutions

### Issue: I cannot complete assessment by deadline

**Solution:**
1. Notify Nominee Coach immediately
2. Request extension if possible
3. If not possible, request reassignment
4. Do not wait until deadline to communicate

### Issue: I identify a COI after starting review

**Solution:**
1. Stop reviewing immediately
2. Notify Nominee Coach immediately
3. Document recusal
4. Return materials
5. Do not share any assessment information

### Issue: Nomination is incomplete or unclear

**Solution:**
1. Document specific gaps or unclear areas
2. Notify Nominee Coach
3. Do not score incomplete sections
4. Note in comments what's missing

### Issue: I disagree with another assessor's scores

**Solution:**
1. This is normal - independent assessment means different perspectives
2. If Nominee Coach requests moderation, participate constructively
3. Explain your rationale
4. Be open to discussion
5. Focus on evidence, not opinions

### Issue: Nominee asks me about their scores

**Solution:**
1. Do not share scores directly with nominee
2. Refer them to Nominee Coach
3. If providing Stage 2 support, focus on improvement, not scores

## Scoring Tips

### General Principles

1. **Be objective:** Score based on evidence, not assumptions
2. **Be consistent:** Apply the same standards across all nominations
3. **Be constructive:** Provide feedback that helps nominees improve
4. **Be thorough:** Review all materials before scoring
5. **Be fair:** Consider the context and category

### What Makes a Winner?

Remember the Award Credo - winners demonstrate:
- **Beyond Competence → Excellence**
- **Beyond Compliance → Impact**
- **Beyond Project Success → Industry Contribution**
- **Beyond Standard Practice → Innovation**
- **Beyond Completion → Legacy**

**The deciding factor:** Would we want to publish this as a case study? Would others learn from this?

### Red Flags (Low Scores)

- Missing or incomplete evidence
- No measurable impact
- Standard approach, no innovation
- No industry contribution
- Poor risk management
- Integrity concerns
- No people development investment

### Green Flags (High Scores)

- Strong measurable impact and transformation
- Significant innovation and uniqueness
- Active knowledge sharing and industry contribution
- Comprehensive risk management
- Strong integrity controls
- Investment in people development (5%+)
- Case study worthy
