# Conflict of Interest (COI) Policy

## Definition

A Conflict of Interest (COI) exists when a reviewer's, juror's, or volunteer's personal, professional, financial, familial, or legal interests could compromise or appear to compromise their objectivity, independence, or professional judgment in evaluating nominations for the PBF Contractor of the Year award.

## Core Principle

All evaluators must maintain complete independence and objectivity. Contractors must not be legally, financially, or otherwise beholden to clients in a manner that could influence evaluation decisions.

## Types of Conflicts Requiring Disclosure and Recusal

### 1. Financial Interests

* Direct or indirect ownership, investment, or financial stake in the nominated contractor organization, client organization, or their parent/subsidiary entities
* Outstanding loans, guarantees, or financial obligations between the evaluator and the contractor or client
* Equity holdings, stock options, or other securities in related entities
* Consulting fees, retainers, or other compensation from the contractor or client (current or within the past 3 years)
* Any financial arrangement that could create an appearance of bias or dependency

### 2. Professional and Employment Relationships

* Current or former employment with the nominated contractor organization or client organization (within the past 3 years)
* Current or former consulting, advisory, or board positions with either organization
* Ongoing or recent (within 3 years) business partnerships or joint ventures
* Subcontractor relationships or vendor relationships that create dependency or influence
* Any professional relationship that could create an obligation or expectation of favorable treatment

### 3. Family and Personal Relationships

* Family members (spouse, domestic partner, children, parents, siblings, in-laws) who are employed by, own, or have significant financial interests in the contractor or client organization
* Close personal friendships or relationships that could reasonably be expected to influence judgment
* Romantic or intimate relationships with key personnel from either organization
* Any familial or personal relationship that could create pressure, obligation, or appearance of bias

### 4. Legal and Contractual Obligations

* Ongoing legal disputes or litigation with the contractor or client
* Non-disclosure agreements, non-compete agreements, or other contractual restrictions that could limit objectivity
* Indemnification agreements or liability arrangements that create dependency
* Any legal or contractual relationship that could compromise independence or create an appearance of bias

### 5. Organizational Affiliations

* Membership in the same professional associations, clubs, or organizations where close relationships exist
* Shared board memberships or governance roles
* Academic or research collaborations that create ongoing relationships
* Any organizational affiliation that could create pressure or obligation

### 6. Other Situations Creating Appearance of Bias

* Prior negative or positive interactions that could influence judgment
* Gifts, entertainment, or favors received from contractor or client representatives (beyond nominal value)
* Any situation where a reasonable person would question the evaluator's independence or objectivity

## Disclosure Requirements

**Initial Disclosure:** All reviewers, judges, and volunteers must complete a written COI disclosure form before participating in any evaluation activities. This disclosure must be updated:

* Annually during participation
* Immediately upon becoming aware of any new potential conflict
* Before each evaluation cycle or assignment

**Disclosure Content:** The disclosure must include:

* Identification of the contractor and/or client organization(s) involved
* Nature of the relationship or interest
* Duration and current status of the relationship
* Financial value (if applicable)
* Any other relevant details

## Recusal Procedures

**Automatic Recusal:** Evaluators must immediately recuse themselves from evaluating any nomination where:

* They have a direct financial interest in the outcome
* A family member has a significant role or financial interest
* They have an ongoing legal or contractual obligation that could influence judgment
* They have a current employment or consulting relationship

**Discretionary Recusal:** Evaluators should recuse themselves when:

* They have a close personal relationship that could reasonably be perceived as influencing judgment
* They have had significant prior professional interactions that could create bias
* Any situation exists where their independence could reasonably be questioned

**Recusal Process:**

1. Evaluator identifies potential conflict and immediately notifies the Program Secretariat
2. Program Secretariat documents the recusal and assigns an alternate evaluator
3. Recused evaluator is excluded from all discussions, scoring, and decisions related to the conflicted nomination
4. All recusals are recorded in the evaluation audit trail
5. Quorum requirements are verified after recusals

## Consequences of Non-Compliance

Failure to disclose conflicts of interest or violation of this policy may result in:

* Immediate removal from the evaluation panel
* Disqualification of affected evaluations
* Review and potential reversal of decisions
* Exclusion from future participation
* Other measures as deemed appropriate by PBF governance

## Acknowledgment

All evaluators must sign an acknowledgment confirming they have:

* Read and understood this COI policy
* Disclosed all actual and potential conflicts
* Committed to maintaining independence and objectivity
* Agreed to recuse themselves when conflicts arise
* Understood the consequences of non-compliance
