# Evaluation Framework

**Status:** Draft - Subject to change

This framework defines the criteria and scoring approach for evaluating PBF Contractor of the Year Award nominations.

---

## Scoring Scale and Aggregation

* 0–5 scale per criterion
* Weights on 0–100 basis
* Minimum two independent reviews per nomination
* Moderated average calculated
* Inter-rater reliability tracked

---

## Criteria (aligned to cross‑corporate PBM)

### 1. Project success (outcomes)

* Delivery performance vs. baseline (scope, time, cost, quality), benefits realization, and handover effectiveness.
* **Measurable impact and transformation** - Quantifiable outcomes, transformation evidence, long-term benefits, ROI/value demonstration (from "Impact Measurement & Transformation" section).

### 2. Agility & adaptability

* Responsiveness to change; effective change control/requirements management under the chosen contracting model.

**Note on Contract Types:** This framework applies to both fixed-price and Time & Material (T&M) contracts. For T&M contracts, evaluators should consider:

* Billing frequency and cycle alignment with contractor cash flow needs
* Rate transparency and justification
* Client cost visibility and control mechanisms
* Effectiveness of change management processes that accommodate flexible scope and shifting priorities
* How the contractor balances adaptability with maintaining project objectives and budget awareness

### 3. Commercial model (liquidity impact)

* Price realism; milestone cash‑flow; advances/retentions; effect on both parties' liquidity and solvency during execution.

### 4. Legal soundness

* Applicable law, jurisdiction, warranty/liability limits, privity, completeness; suitability of contracting approach to collaboration model.

### 5. Interface and governance

* Clarity of obligations at the customer–contractor boundary (customer provisions/enabling services vs contractor deliverables/timelines); escalation paths; decision rights; cross‑corporate governance structures.

### 6. Risk management (cross‑corporate)

* Identification of cross‑party risks (bankruptcy propagation, subcontractor dependencies, IP/data, export/GDPR); mitigations and contingency.
* Cross-corporate risk management covers the identification, monitoring, and mitigation of risks arising from dependencies and collaborations between organizations. This includes:

    * Financial and commercial exposure (solvency, payment risk, contractual obligations)
    * Operational and supply chain continuity (subcontractor performance, logistics resilience)
    * Information and intellectual property protection (data privacy, export controls, IP ownership)
    * Cybersecurity and technology interconnectivity (third-party systems, data exchange security)
    * Regulatory and compliance alignment (GDPR, Environmental, Social and Governance, trade, and labour laws)
    * Reputational, ethical, and governance consistency across partner networks
    * Geopolitical and environmental resilience in multi-region operations
    

    Mitigation measures include contractual controls, audit rights, compliance monitoring, diversification, continuity planning, and crisis coordination mechanisms

### 7. People Development

* Investment in staff development as a percentage of net income/profit. Categories: 0%, 0-5%, or 5%+. This metric reflects the contractor's commitment to developing team capabilities (technical, organizational, or commercial) that benefit both customer and contractor. Higher investment in people development typically correlates with better project outcomes and customer satisfaction.

### 8. Team and business acumen (Integrity and conduct)

* Leadership and interpersonal effectiveness across organizations; ability to balance customer satisfaction with profitability and liquidity (profit‑center management by contractor PMs).
* Adherence to an explicit cross‑corporate code of conduct; anti‑corruption stance; transparency and documentation discipline.

### 9. Innovation & Industry Advancement (NEW)

**This criterion evaluates what makes the project exemplary and distinguishes winners from competent performers.**

Evaluates:

* **Innovation & Uniqueness** - Novel approaches, pioneering techniques, challenges to industry norms, creation of new best practices (from "What Makes This Exemplary?" section)
* **Industry Contribution** - How the project advances the field; knowledge sharing; thought leadership; replicability (from "Lessons Learned & Industry Contribution" section)
* **Broader Impact** - Beyond project success, what changed? Transformation of client's business, industry, or community (from "What Makes This Exemplary?" section)
* **Case Study Worthiness** - Would this make a valuable case study? Is it worth publishing and sharing with others?

**Performance Descriptors:**

* **0:** Not demonstrated / missing evidence
* **1:** Poor – Standard approach, no innovation, no industry contribution
* **2:** Fair – Some novel elements, minor improvements, limited knowledge sharing
* **3:** Good – Solid innovation, some industry relevance, basic knowledge sharing
* **4:** Very Good – Significant innovation, industry-relevant, active knowledge sharing, replicable practices
* **5:** Excellent – Groundbreaking approach, advances the profession, extensive knowledge sharing, highly replicable, case study worthy

**Note:** Winners should typically score 4-5 on this criterion, demonstrating they did something uniquely valuable that others can learn from.

## Cross‑Corporate Anchors (what to look for)

* Explicit distinction of internal vs customer projects; treatment of multi‑party interfaces where projects break through organizational walls.
* Suitability for mixed internal/external delivery models (extended workbench vs fully outsourced); impact on PM responsibilities and risk profiles.
* Contract completeness and enforceability: coverage of law/jurisdiction, warranty/liability, capacity and signatures, notice provisions, change control, dispute resolution.
* Contractor financial resilience: liquidity and cash‑flow profile; insolvency risk and buffers; sensitivity of the payment schedule to cost burn.

---

## Performance Descriptors (examples)

* **Delivery:** Variance vs baseline (SPI/CPI, milestone adherence, scope control); schedule and cost realism; measurable impact and transformation evidence.
* **Commercial model:** Milestone plan supports liquidity of both parties; clear retention/advance logic; fair risk‑sharing.
* **Legal:** Contract is complete and enforceable; terms match collaboration model; clear privity and subcontract flow‑downs.
* **Interface/governance:** Crisp allocation of obligations; timely escalation; clear decision rights and PBMO/PMO roles.
* **Risk:** Cross‑corporate risk register with owner per risk and mitigations; insolvency cascade addressed.
* **People Development:** Evidence of investment in staff development (training, certifications, knowledge sharing); percentage of profit invested; impact on project delivery and customer satisfaction.
* **Integrity:** COI controls; transparency; documentation discipline in line with contract performance guidance.
* **Team/acumen:** Evidence of profit‑center management, claim prevention, and win‑win negotiation behavior.
* **Innovation & Industry Advancement:** Novel approaches demonstrated; industry contribution through knowledge sharing; measurable broader impact; case study worthiness; replicable practices that advance the profession.

---

## What Makes Winners Truly Exemplary?

Winners should demonstrate:

1. **Beyond Competence → Excellence** - Not just "did the job well," but did something uniquely valuable
2. **Beyond Compliance → Impact** - Not just "had good processes," but created measurable, lasting change
3. **Beyond Project Success → Industry Contribution** - Not just "client was happy," but advanced the profession and shared knowledge
4. **Beyond Standard Practice → Innovation** - Not just "followed best practices," but created new best practices
5. **Beyond Completion → Legacy** - Not just "finished the project," but left lasting impact and transformed relationships

**The deciding factor:** Would we want to publish this as a case study? Would others learn from this? Is this truly exemplary, not just competent?
