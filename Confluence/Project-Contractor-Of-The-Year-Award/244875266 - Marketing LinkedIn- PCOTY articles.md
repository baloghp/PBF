### Oliver's topic suggestions — series

**Defining what the award stands for** (Anticipation)
1. What "excellence on the contractor side" actually means — naming the discipline of Project Business Management, not just delivery competences.
2. Traits separating a great project contractor from a merely competent supplier: trust, transparency, contract fluency, fair risk-sharing.
3. Why there's an award for contractors at all — the underappreciated seller side of cross-corporate project work.
4. What the jury looks for: the criteria, unpacked one dimension per post.
5. Nomination mechanics and timeline — who can nominate, what evidence helps, key dates for 2027.

**The economics and stakes** (Anticipation)
1. Share of the global economy that runs through project business (corridor paper) — contractors as an invisible engine.
2. The cost to customers of picking the wrong contractor — late, over budget, litigated to a standstill.
3. Cash flow and liquidity as a contractor discipline, not an accounting afterthought (ties to Cash Radar).
4. Why the lowest bid is so often the most expensive contractor.

**Craft and behaviour worth celebrating** (Launch)
1. Bidding and tendering with integrity — honest proposal vs lowball trap.
2. Claims management as collaboration rather than ambush.
3. Handling change requests across the corporate boundary without eroding the relationship.
4. The charter as a shared instrument between customer and contractor (ties to Charter Forge).
5. Risk distribution across the customer–contractor line — who carries what, and why.
6. Contract types and what each signals about the relationship.
7. Cross-corporate, cross-cultural collaboration when two organizations' cultures collide.

**Recognition and community** (Launch)
1. Profiles of past winners / exemplary contractor behaviours (anonymized vignettes if needed).
2. What professionalization looks like on the contractor side — the PBP credential as a marker.
3. Reader-prompt: "Describe the best contractor you ever worked with in one sentence."

**Cost-of-inaction (signature close)** (Launch)
1. Organizations that keep treating contractors as interchangeable — and pay for it in failed projects.
2. The reputational cost of being a contractor nobody nominates.
